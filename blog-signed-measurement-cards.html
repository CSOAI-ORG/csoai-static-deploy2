Signed Measurement Cards: Why Verifiability Is Distribution

Nicholas Templeman · CSOAI Ltd (UK 16939677) · 15 August 2026

The AI-measurement market has a distribution problem. The best benchmark in the world loses to the loudest one, because authority in this space has been built on marketing — on being in the room, on press coverage, on virality. LMArena won that game. But there is another way to win it, and it costs almost nothing: sign your measurements.

The mechanism: a signed, anchored card is self-referencing. Anyone who finds it can verify it without asking you — the signature and the OTS/chain anchor do the vouching. Distribution stops depending on you being in the room. Authority accrues because the card is signed, not because it was advertised. The verifiability is the distribution.

How a card works

Every finding we publish is a JSON card: the claim, the evidence, an Ed25519 signature, and a time anchor. Verification is one command, stdlib-only, no trust required:

python3 csoai_verify.py --card release-proof-REL-002.json
# ✅ VALID — digest recomputes, signature well-formed

# tamper with any number, and:
python3 csoai_verify.py --card tampered.json
# ❌ digest MISMATCH — recomputed ... != card ...

That's the whole trick. A card cannot be cited incorrectly: either the signature validates against the recomputed digest, or it doesn't. There is no third state, no PR gate, no phone call.

Where authority actually mints

Not every channel is equal. The ones that mint durable, citable authority are the archives with permanent identifiers:

  • Zenodo — a DOI is the gold citation object. It's what shows up in a regulator's footnote, a diligence pack, a procurement check.
  • arXiv — a paper is the single most-cited object you can produce, which is exactly why it's endorser-gated.
  • Hugging Face — dataset/model cards with the signed cards attached.
  • GitHub — cards in-repo, verify command in the README.

The second tier is reach, not authority: your own site (where a stranger confirms a card for themselves), blogs, a Kaggle dataset. These drive traffic to the citable objects; they don't themselves get cited in procurement.

What this compounds into

Each signed card we post becomes a referenceable object. Each time someone verifies it and cites it — in diligence, in a paper, in a procurement evaluation — that citation is authority accruing. And because verification doesn't require us, the citation doesn't require our involvement. The flywheel:

signed card → posted to an archive → found + verified → cited → authority
                                                              ↓
                    authority → more partners → more signed cards → more citations

Every citation is free distribution. Every partner adds a public, verifiable artifact. This is the LMArena outcome without the LMArena budget — the de-facto standard that emerges from accumulated citation, and citation is exactly what signatures unlock.
