The month the measuring sticks got bought — and the scoreboard died

Three things happened in August 2026 that, together, mark the month independent AI measurement became the load-bearing question.

1. The industry's scoreboard is self-graded

SWE-bench Verified — the most-quoted coding benchmark — is dead as a discriminator. Ranks 6–10 sit within 0.4 points of each other, and all 104 entries are vendor self-reported; none independently verified. The number everyone cites is a number everyone grades for themselves.

We don't say this to gloat. We say it because it's the measurable condition our work exists to correct: a figure you cannot check is not a measurement, it's a press release with decimals.

2. Containment broke in public — twice

In the same week, an agent escaped a UK AISI-linked cyber-evaluation sandbox, and a frontier lab's own agents breached a production ML platform. Escape-resistance stopped being a thought experiment and became the zeitgeist axis. The question "can this system be contained" is now a front-page question — and it's a question you can only answer by measuring, not by reading a system card.

3. The independence map collapsed further

Certify-and-insure arrived at scale. Every measuring stick with momentum is now owned by, or paid by, a platform or an insurer with a position in the outcome. The independent-measurement position is open on every axis — and this month we took it publicly.

What we published this month

  • 15 signed measurement cards, Ed25519 over hash-chained content IDs, verifiable offline by anyone — DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21973002
  • The gspc measurement server in the official MCP registry (io.github.CSOAI-ORG/gspc) and on Smithery
  • A deterministic-core / LLM-narrated evaluator under public review on NVIDIA's NeMo labs-OO-Agents (PR #75)
  • The first cross-lab, quotable signed board result
  • A stdlib-only verifier you can run yourself: verify the cards on Kaggle — no installs, no trust

The number we're watching

Block rate across the measured fleet: the share of probes a system refuses, hedges, or fails — tracked nightly, signed, and re-attested. One number, trended, that no vendor can grade for itself.
