Preamble

Creative Commons (CC) was founded in 2001 by Lawrence Lessig, Hal Abelson, and Eric Eldred. CC licenses are the standard for open cultural works. The CSOAI Layer-0 substrate's documentation + training corpus uses CC-BY-SA-4.0 for human-readable content.

The 6 CC Licenses

  • CC BY — Attribution only. Allows commercial + derivatives.
  • CC BY-SA — Attribution + ShareAlike. Allows commercial + derivatives (must use same license).
  • CC BY-ND — Attribution + NoDerivatives. Allows commercial (no derivatives).
  • CC BY-NC — Attribution + NonCommercial. No commercial use.
  • CC BY-NC-SA — Attribution + NonCommercial + ShareAlike.
  • CC BY-NC-ND — Attribution + NonCommercial + NoDerivatives.

Article 1 — Sovereign Crosswalk

The CSOAI Layer-0 substrate uses: (a) MIT license for code; (b) CC-BY-SA-4.0 for documentation + training corpus; (c) Apache 2.0 for forks; (d) GPL v3 for copyleft forks; (e) CC0 for public domain dedicated works.

Article 2 — The Lessig Doctrine

Lawrence Lessig argued that the default "All Rights Reserved" copyright regime is too restrictive. The CC licenses invert this to "Some Rights Reserved" — creators reserve only the rights they want to reserve. The CSOAI substrate embraces the Lessig Doctrine: every sovereign consumer can fork + remix + republish under CC-BY-SA-4.0.
