Preamble

The CSOAI AI Ethics Charter establishes the moral, technical, and sovereign principles that govern the design, deployment, and oversight of artificial intelligence systems within the sovereign substrate. The Charter synthesises the Asilomar AI Principles (2017), the OECD AI Principles (2019), the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021), the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689, in force 2 August 2026), the UK AI Bill 2026, and the sovereign ethics of CSOAI Ltd (UK 16939677).

Foundational Documents

  • Asilomar AI Principles (2017): 23 principles in three categories — Research Issues, Ethics and Values, Longer-term Issues. Notable: "AI Safety", "Failure Transparency", "Judicial Transparency", "Responsibility", "Value Alignment", "Personal Privacy", "Liberty and Privacy".
  • OECD AI Principles (2019): five principles for responsible stewardship of trustworthy AI — Inclusive growth, sustainable development and well-being; Human-centred values and fairness; Transparency and explainability; Robustness, security and safety; Accountability.
  • UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021): 10 principles — proportionality; fairness; non-discrimination; sustainability; right to privacy and data protection; human oversight and determination; transparency and explainability; responsibility and accountability; awareness and literacy; multi-stakeholder and adaptive governance and collaboration.
  • EU AI Act Article 4a: AI literacy requirement — providers and deployers shall ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff.
  • EU AI Act Article 14: Human oversight — high-risk AI systems shall be designed to allow effective human oversight during the period of use.
  • EU AI Act Article 50: Transparency obligations — providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed to inform those persons that they are interacting with an AI system, except where this is obvious.
  • UK AI Bill 2026: 5 principles — safety, security, robustness, transparency, contestability. Applies to general-purpose AI models trained with > 10^25 FLOPs.

Specific Cases

ProPublica COMPAS recidivism (2016): ProPublica investigation revealed that COMPAS (Correctional Offender Management Profiling for Alternative Sanctions), used in US courts to predict recidivism, was biased against Black defendants. Counterfactual Fairness Failure Rate 23.2% for Black, 8.6% for white. Result: public awareness of algorithmic bias.

Amazon AI hiring (2018): Amazon's AI recruiting tool was found to discriminate against women, downgrading resumes containing the word "women's" (e.g. "women's chess club captain"). Discontinued.

Apple Card gender discrimination (2019): David Heinemeier Hansson reported the Apple Card algorithm gave him 20× the credit limit of his wife, despite her higher credit score. New York State DFS investigation found the algorithm discriminated on gender. Result: algorithmic accountability spotlight.

EU AI Act negotiations (2021–2024): trilogue among EU Parliament, Council, Commission produced the final Regulation 2024/1689 on 13 March 2024. Entered force 1 August 2024; high-risk provisions apply from 2 August 2026.

Modern Application (2026)

The CSOAI AI Ethics Charter is implemented through:

  1. Care Floor 0.95: minimum acceptable care score for any AI agent; substrate enforces during BFT deliberation.
  2. Article 14 human oversight: every high-risk AI decision requires a 4-eyes human review before execution.
  3. Article 50 transparency: every sovereign citizen knows when they interact with an AI agent.
  4. SIGIL attribution: every AI decision is logged with model + input + basis + version, signed Ed25519.
  5. Asilomar Principle 23 (Common Good): "Superintelligence should only be developed in the service of widely shared ethical ideals, and for the benefit of all humanity rather than one state or organization." Substrate enforces via the BFT council's 33 nodes, no single actor controls.
