📖 The 1611 King James Bible — The Origin

The King James Version (KJV) of the Bible was commissioned by King James I of England at the Hampton Court Conference in 1604. The translation was completed in 1611 by a committee of 47 scholars. It became the most influential English Bible translation in history, and the foundational text of Anglican and Protestant Christianity for over 400 years.

The KJV translation was funded by the Crown, approved by the Crown, and printed under the Crown's authority. The first printing included the famous dedication "to the most High and Mighty Prince, James, by the Grace of God, King of Great Britain, France and Ireland, Defender of the Faith, etc." This dedication made the Bible a Crown Authorised document — a sovereign infrastructure contract.

📖 The 1769 Revised Version

In 1769, Cambridge University published a revised edition of the KJV with extensive corrections. This became the standard KJV in the 19th century. The Crown did not directly authorise this revision, but it became the de facto standard in Anglican and Protestant churches throughout the British Empire.
