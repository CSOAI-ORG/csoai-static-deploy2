2. The Problem (Why Sovereignty Now)

2.1 Foreign dependency

UK defence AI capability is currently 95%+ licensed from US vendors (Palantir Foundry powers much of UK defence data integration; Anduril Lattice is shortlisted for UK C2). These platforms:

  • Are closed-source (no MOD audit of weights, gradients, or training data)
  • Are subject to US CLOUD Act (US government can compel data access)
  • Are subject to US export controls (ITAR/EAR restrictions on updates)
  • Cannot be AUKUS-interoperable in a "Five Eyes data sharing" context where UK data sovereignty is required

2.2 Cyber-physical attack surface

The 2025 Salt Typhoon attack on US telecoms revealed that closed-source proprietary systems are prime targets for state-aligned APTs. Defence AI platforms containing C2, targeting, and ISR data MUST be open to UK national CERT inspection. Closed-source systems = unauditable = un-auditable-by-design.

2.3 The acquisition trap

UK MOD pays ~£50M/yr to Palantir for the Foundry Defence stack. After 5 years: £250M sunk, no exit, no UK jobs created, no UK IP retained, no UK cyber resilience built. The alternative sovereign stack: £2.5M one-time + £4.5M/yr OSS maintenance + UK jobs = 90% TCO reduction over 10 years.
