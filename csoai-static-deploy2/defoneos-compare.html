Part 2 — Where Each Competitor Wins

Palantir Foundry

  • Wins on: 20 years of field deployment, 100+ government customers, Gotham/AIP mature workflows, deep US intel community integration.
  • Loses on: Closed source, US CLOUD Act, $50M+/yr pricing, vendor lock-in, no UK IP, no UK cyber resilience, no AUKUS data sovereignty.
  • What DEFONEOS learns: Foundry's data integration patterns (ontology, semantic layer) — replicated via open ontology + BFT council.

Anduril Lattice

  • Wins on: Modern UX, hardware integration (Dive-LD, Fury, Ghost), $1.5B+ in annual revenue, US SOCOM/UK MARLAN demos.
  • Loses on: Hardware-heavy (requires you to buy Anduril drones), closed source, US jurisdiction, no cyber/spectrum/cognitive domains, no JSP 936.
  • What DEFONEOS learns: Hardware integration via Mava (PX4-compatible) — DEFONEOS supports Anduril-style hardware if customer has it.

Helsing (EU/DE)

  • Wins on: EU sovereign (alternative to US CLOUD Act), strong AI/ML pedigree (German AI出身), German MoD contracts, ethical positioning.
  • Loses on: Closed source, €2M+/yr pricing, only 1 domain (air targeting), no UK sovereign protection (DE jurisdiction post-Brexit still EU), no AUKUS.
  • What DEFONEOS learns: EU sovereignty narrative resonates — DEFONEOS extends it to UK sovereignty for Five Eyes data sharing.

Shield AI Hivemind

  • Wins on: Mature autonomous drone swarms (5+ years), US DoD contracts, Nova-class drones fielded.
  • Loses on: Drone-only (no broader OS), closed source, US CLOUD Act, no cyber/EW/space domains.
  • What DEFONEOS learns: Mava (PX4-compat) swarm logic + Hivemind-style behaviour trees — DEFONEOS integrates, not replicates.
