🗺️ Procurement Roadmap Overview

The UK MOD procurement pipeline has six critical gateways for AI vendors. Each builds on the previous — skip one and you stall. Here is the sequence that works:

Phase 1 — Months 1–2

DSP (Defence Supplier Portal) Registration

Register on the Defence Sourcing Portal (formerly Defence Contracts Online). This is the mandatory entry point — all MOD contracts above £10K are advertised here. Without a DSP account you cannot bid on any defence work.

Phase 2 — Months 2–3

Cyber Essentials Plus Certification

Obtain Cyber Essentials Plus certification. Basic Cyber Essentials is self-assessed; Plus requires an independent audit. MOD requires Plus for contracts handling any sensitive data. Cost: £1,500–£5,000 depending on assessor and org size.

Phase 3 — Months 3–6

Security Clearance (SC / DV)

Apply for Security Check (SC) clearance for key personnel. SC is the baseline for most MOD contracts; Developed Vetting (DV) is needed for Top Secret work. Processing takes 3–6 months. The sponsoring MOD unit initiates the application.

Phase 4 — Months 4–8

JSP 936 Compliance

Implement JSP 936: MOD Information & Cyber Security controls. This is the MOD's mandatory security standard — it maps to ISO 27001 but adds defence-specific requirements around data handling, network segmentation, and incident reporting.

Phase 5 — Months 6–12

DASA Innovation Application

Apply to the Defence and Security Accelerator (DASA) for innovation funding. DASA runs themed competitions (typically £50K–£500K per project) and is the MOD's primary route for novel AI/ML capabilities to enter the defence pipeline.

Phase 6 — Months 12–18

NATO DIANA Accelerator

Apply to NATO DIANA (Defence Innovation Accelerator for the North Atlantic) for access to 30+ NATO nation test centres and dual-use technology funding. DIANA focuses on emerging tech: AI, quantum, biotech, energy, space.

📋 1. DSP — Defence Supplier Portal Registration

What It Is

The Defence Sourcing Portal (DSP) is the UK MOD's central procurement platform. All defence contracts above £10,000 are advertised here. Registration is free and mandatory before you can bid on any MOD work.

Cost: Free to register
Timeframe: 2–4 weeks for approval

Registration Requirements

  • UK-registered company (Companies House number required)
  • DUNS number (free from Dun & Bradstreet)
  • NAICS/SIC codes for your capability area
  • Company insurance details (public liability, professional indemnity)
  • Financial accounts or evidence of financial standing
  • Named contact with authority to bid on behalf of the company

How to Register

  1. Visit contracts.mod.uk — the Defence Sourcing Portal
  2. Create a supplier account with your Companies House details
  3. Complete the Supplier Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ)
  4. Select relevant commodity codes (AI/ML: typically under IT services and R&D)
  5. Submit and wait for MOD approval (2–4 weeks)
💡 Tip: Also register on Find a Tender (the post-Brexit replacement for OJEU). Many MOD opportunities are cross-listed there.

🔒 2. Cyber Essentials Plus

Why It Matters for MOD

Since 2024, all MOD contracts handling government data require Cyber Essentials Plus as a minimum. Basic Cyber Essentials (self-assessment) is no longer sufficient for defence work.

Cost: £1,500–£5,000 (assessor + remediation)
Timeframe: 4–8 weeks

🔐 3. Security Clearance (SC / DV)

Clearance Levels

UK government security clearance is managed by the United Kingdom Security Vetting (UKSV) unit. You cannot self-apply — an MOD sponsor must initiate the process.

Cost: Free (government-funded)
Timeframe: SC: 3–6 months · DV: 6–12 months
LevelAccess GrantedTypical AI Vendor NeedProcessing Time
Baseline Check (BC)Government assets, no classifiedAdministrative roles2–4 weeks
Counter-Terrorist Check (CTC)Controlled assets near public accessOn-site demo roles4–8 weeks
Security Check (SC)Secret and belowMost MOD AI contracts3–6 months
Developed Vetting (DV)Top Secret and all aboveClassified AI systems, ISR6–12 months

Key Personnel to Clear First

  • Lead Engineer / CTO — needs SC minimum for technical discussions
  • Programme Manager — needs SC for contract management access
  • Data Protection Officer — if handling classified datasets
  • Any on-site staff — BC minimum, SC if entering secure areas
⚠️ Warning: SC clearance takes 3–6 months. Start the process immediately after DSP registration — do not wait until you win a contract. An MOD sponsor (the contracting unit) must initiate the application via UKSV.

🛡️ 4. JSP 936 — MOD Information & Cyber Security

What It Is

JSP 936 (Joint Service Publication 936) is the MOD's mandatory information security framework. It supersedes and extends ISO 27001 with defence-specific controls for data classification, network architecture, incident response, and supply chain security.

Cost: £10,000–£50,000 (implementation + audit)
Timeframe: 3–6 months for initial compliance

JSP 936 Key Sections for AI Vendors

SectionFocusAI-Specific Requirements
JSP 936 Part 1Information Security PolicyAI model governance policy, data classification for training sets
JSP 936 Part 2Risk ManagementAI risk assessment (bias, adversarial attacks, model drift)
JSP 936 Part 3Access ControlRBAC for ML pipelines, model access logging
JSP 936 Part 4System DevelopmentSecure ML development lifecycle, reproducibility
JSP 936 Part 8Incident ManagementAI-specific incident reporting (model compromise, data exfiltration)
JSP 936 Part 12Supply ChainThird-party model provenance, dependency auditing

Compliance Pathway

  1. Gap Analysis — map your existing ISO 27001 controls against JSP 936 requirements
  2. Remediation Plan — address defence-specific gaps (network segmentation, data labelling, incident escalation to MOD CSIRT)
  3. Implementation — deploy controls, document in a System Security Plan (SSP)
  4. Accreditation — engage a CLAS (CESG Listed Adviser Scheme) consultant to review
  5. Approval — the MOD accreditor signs off; you receive a letter of accreditation
💡 Tip: If you already hold ISO 27001, you are roughly 60% of the way to JSP 936. The main additions are defence data handling (OFFICIAL, SECRET classifications), MOD incident reporting chains, and supply chain provenance for AI components.

🚀 5. DASA — Defence and Security Accelerator

What It Is

DASA is the MOD's innovation arm — it funds novel defence and security technologies through themed competitions. It is the primary route for AI/ML startups to enter the MOD supply chain without prior defence contracts.

Cost: Free to apply
Timeframe: Competition cycles: 8–16 weeks · Funding: £50K–£500K per project

How DASA Works

  1. Themed Competitions — MOD publishes challenge areas (e.g., "AI for ISR", "Autonomous Systems", "Cyber Defence")
  2. Proposal Submission — you submit a 10–15 page proposal via the DASA platform
  3. Assessment — MOD technical experts evaluate innovation potential, feasibility, and defence impact
  4. Funding — successful proposals receive phased funding (typically 3 phases: feasibility → prototype → demonstration)
  5. Transition — successful Phase 3 projects are introduced to MOD procurement units

Current Open Competitions (as of September 2026)

  • DASA Open Call — rolling submissions, always open
  • DASA Themed Competitions — time-limited challenges with specific focus areas
💡 Tip: DASA does not require Cyber Essentials or SC clearance to apply. It is deliberately low-barrier. Use DASA as your entry point — then pursue Cyber Essentials and clearance once you have MOD funding and a sponsor.

🌍 6. NATO DIANA — Defence Innovation Accelerator

What It Is

NATO DIANA (Defence Innovation Accelerator for the North Atlantic) connects dual-use technology startups with NATO nation test centres, defence customers, and innovation funding. DIANA focuses on seven technology areas including AI, quantum, biotech, and space.

Cost: Free to apply
Timeframe: Annual application cycle · Programme: 6–9 months

DIANA Application Process

  1. Expression of Interest — submit via diana.nato.int during annual call
  2. Selection — NATO evaluates dual-use potential and defence relevance
  3. Accelerator Programme — 6–9 months of mentorship, test centre access, and seed funding
  4. NATO Network — graduates gain access to 30+ NATO nation procurement networks
💡 Tip: DIANA does not require prior NATO or MOD contracts. It is designed as an entry point. UK companies apply through the UK DIANA national point of contact at the MOD's Defence Innovation unit.

⚡ Recommended Action Plan for AI Startups

Month 1: Foundation

  • Register on the Defence Sourcing Portal
  • Get a DUNS number from Dun & Bradstreet
  • Begin Cyber Essentials Plus self-assessment
  • Identify an MOD sponsor unit for SC clearance

Month 2–3: Security Baseline

  • Complete Cyber Essentials Plus certification
  • Submit SC clearance applications for key personnel
  • Begin JSP 936 gap analysis (if pursuing system integration contracts)
  • Submit first DASA Open Call proposal

Month 4–6: Active Bidding

  • Bid on first MOD contracts via DSP
  • Engage CLAS consultant for JSP 936 accreditation
  • Monitor NATO DIANA application cycle
  • Attend DSTL industry days and DASA showcase events

Month 7–12: Scale

  • Complete JSP 936 accreditation
  • Receive SC clearances — begin classified work
  • Submit NATO DIANA application
  • Explore Defence and Security Industry partnerships
