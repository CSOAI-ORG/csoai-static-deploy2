We respond precisely to the asked questions: **what risks consumers feel exposed to, to what extent,
and where they will / will not tolerate risk in exchange for AI benefits** — plus how the four

## 1. Who we are
CSOAI LTD (UK #16939677) — an independent AI-governance **measurement** body. We issue signed,
buyer-side measurement evidence. **We measure; we do not certify.** (Grammar-required framing.)

## 2. The evidence gap we answer (directly relevant to the CfI)
The CfI asks regulators to assess AI-linked **consumer harm and tolerance**. But across the four
DRCF remits (online safety, data protection, competition, consumer) the same object is missing:
- Regulators hold **no neutral lab relationships**; they rely on first-party self-reports.
- Self-reports are **unsigned** and not independently replayable — so a claim of "low consumer risk"
  or "fair, transparent, non-discriminatory AI" has no third-party anchor.

That is the gap a measurement body fills: a **signed, third-party, re-measurable evidence record** of
what an AI system actually scored on the harm axes consumers care about — transparency, fairness,
privacy, safety, accountability, and (for consumer protection) the sovereignty/continuity of the
service the consumer is exposed to.

## 3. Our contribution to the CfI (evidence, not opinion)
- **Signed measurement cards** (RFC 9943-conformant, ed25519) — score vector + environment
  commitment + replay root; the consumer-harm-relevant axes are independently replayable by any
  stranger offline.
- **Independence + COI screening** (SB 315-pattern, pre-compliant): every card states its
  independence posture; the scored party never pays.
- **Buyer/insurer/regulator-pays only** — the regulator (or a consumer-advocacy/insurer party)
  commissions the measurement; the deployer never pays for its own score. This is the clean
  answer to "who measures the AI that touches consumers" without a captive-lab conflict.

## 4. Specific answers to the CfI's core question
**"To what extent are consumers prepared to tolerate risk in exchange for AI benefits?"**
We do not opinionate on the threshold — we provide the **instrument** to measure it in a
re-appraisable way: a reproducible score of fairness/transparency/privacy/safety per system, and a
public honest register (MEASURED / REPORTED / UNMEASURED) so the tolerance question can be
answered from evidence rather than vendor self-presentation.

## 5. Asks (as a neutral party, clearly non-preferential)
1. A **standing channel** for signed-artifact evidence-sharing across the four DRCF regulators
   (so a raw, replayable score can be read by Ofcom, ICO, CMA and FCA from the same record).
2. **Observation/participation** in cross-regulator AI-readiness workstreams — as a measurement
   body, not as a stakeholder with a commercial position.
