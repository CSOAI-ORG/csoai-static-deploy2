Speculative Hypotheses
TL;DR
     The n_eff decorrelation fix is unblocked: a state-space/linear-attention leg (Mamba-2,
     RWKV-7, Falcon-Mamba, Kimi Linear) can be inferenced today on Apple Silicon via
     llama.cpp/Metal and MLX; CUDA absence is a solvable inconvenience, not a blocker —
     and the cheapest path to a distilled decorrelated leg is a one-off cloud GPU rental
     (RunPod A100 80GB ≈ $1.39/hr), not local training.
     The single highest-leverage move is ProvBench + PQC: C2PA has NO post-quantum
     signature support (exclusively classical ES256/EdDSA/PS256 through v2.4), the enabling
     COSE ML-DSA identifiers now exist (RFC 9964, published 22 May 2026: ML-DSA-44 =
     COSE alg −48, ML-DSA-65 = −49, ML-DSA-87 = −50) but are unadopted by C2PA, and
     C2PA manifests are designed to validate "indefinitely" — making "does your provenance
     survive a signature-algorithm migration?" a genuine, defensible, deterministic
     benchmark dimension nobody else occupies.
     Photonic "light as compute" is NOT reachable for a solo founder now or in ~3 years
     (lab/hyperscaler only); the real, reachable truth is optical interconnect economics, which
     strengthen (not threaten) the small-model+KB thesis. UE5/VWM is a legitimate
     deterministic rendering surface but a judgement-based cognitive "visual world model"
     would fail like routing/quorum did.
Key Findings
Q1 — CUDA/Apple-Silicon kernel gap: PARTIALLY-REAL (real for training kernels,
REFUTED as a blocker for obtaining a decorrelated leg). State-space and linear-attention
models run on Apple Silicon today. mlx-lm has native Mamba v1 and Mamba v2
implementations (contributed by Gökdeniz Gülmez per the mlx-lm ACKNOWLEDGMENTS),
and mlx-community hosts converted Mamba checkpoints (e.g. mamba-1.4b-hf-f32 ).
 llama.cpp lists Mamba, RWKV (6/7), Jamba, and Falcon-Mamba among supported
architectures in GGUF with the Metal backend (device discovery loads CUDA or Metal).
 mamba.py provides a "simple and efficient Mamba (state-space model) in pure PyTorch and
MLX" selective-scan fallback. The genuine gap: the bespoke CUDA/Triton kernels — Mamba-
2's hardware-aware selective scan, and Kimi Linear's KDA/FlashKDA kernels with their
Diagonal-Plus-Low-Rank chunkwise algorithm (arXiv:2510.26692, open-sourced with vLLM
integration, Triton-based) — are CUDA-first and have no optimized Metal port, so you lose the
speed/efficiency advantage on Mac but not the ability to run the model. Verdict: CUDA
absence is a solvable inconvenience. For a one-off distillation/fine-tune to produce an
architecturally-distinct leg, rent cloud GPU (RunPod A100 80GB ≈ $1.39/hr community tier,
per-second billing; H100 PCIe ≈ $2.89/hr); a short distillation run costs single-digit to low-tens
of dollars. Do NOT attempt to train Mamba-2/KDA locally; DO inference the distilled SSM leg
locally afterward via llama.cpp/Metal (GGUF) or MLX. MLX LoRA/QLoRA fine-tuning is real
and unified-memory-native (docs report ≈250 tok/s LoRA on an M1 Max 32 GB; Apple's own
WWDC 2025 numbers claim M5 is 3.5–4× faster than M4 at prompt processing — vendor
figure).
Q2a — Ed25519 is not post-quantum secure: REAL, but the threat model for signatures is
materially different from encryption. Shor's algorithm breaks Ed25519/ECDSA/RSA alike
(migrating ECC→RSA solves nothing — both fall to the same attack). Per NIST IR 8547 initial
public draft (Nov 2024), Table 3: ECDSA and RSA at 112-bit security are "Deprecated after 2030
/ Disallowed after 2035," and EdDSA is "Disallowed after 2035." NSA CNSA 2.0 (updated May
2025): 1 Jan 2027 is the gate by which new National Security Systems acquisitions must
support CNSA 2.0, with software/firmware signing (LMS/XMSS per SP 800-208) reaching
exclusive use by 2030 — 2027 is a "support," not "exclusive-signing," milestone. UK NCSC sets a
three-phase national migration: discovery/plan by 2028, high-priority upgrades 2028–2031, full
migration by 2035. NIST standards: FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA/Dilithium), FIPS
205 (SLH-DSA/SPHINCS+), all 13 Aug 2024; HQC selected March 2025 as a backup KEM.
Crucial nuance for SIGIL: "harvest now, decrypt later" is a confidentiality threat and is largely
IRRELEVANT to signatures — you cannot retroactively decrypt a signature. The real signature
risk is future forgery of long-lived attestation chains: once a cryptographically-relevant
quantum computer (CRQC) exists, an attacker could forge Ed25519 signatures on SIGIL-
attested artifacts and backdate provenance claims. Correct countermeasures: (i) algorithm
agility, (ii) hybrid signatures (classical + PQC), (iii) long-term timestamping — RFC 3161
timestamps and RFC 4998 Evidence Record Syntax, which proves data "already existed
before" a crypto break via hash-tree renewal and timestamp renewal. That is precisely the right
design for a hash-chain attestation system. NIST + 5
Q2b — D-Wave CEO's "quantum breaks Bitcoin" claim: PARTIALLY-REAL but deflated.
What is actually threatened is ECDSA/Schnorr signatures on exposed secp256k1 public keys,
not SHA-256 proof-of-work. Grover's algorithm only quadratically speeds hash search;
commercially-viable Bitcoin PoW attack via Grover is "decades, possibly centuries away."
Bitcoin has partial built-in resistance: unspent P2PKH addresses never reveal the pubkey until
spend, so single-use addresses are not immediate targets. The resource bar is falling fast: Craig
Gidney (Google Quantum AI), arXiv:2505.15917 (21 May 2025) — "a 2048 bit RSA integer could
be factored in less than a week by a quantum computer with less than a million noisy qubits"
(≈1,409 logical / ≈897,864 physical, 4.96 days), a 20× reduction from the Gidney+Ekerå 2019
figure of 20M qubits / 8 hours (same assumptions: 0.1% gate error, 1 μs surface-code cycle, 10 μs
reaction time). A Feb 2026 Iceberg Quantum estimate claims <100k physical qubits using
qLDPC codes. But no such machine exists (Google Willow ≈105 qubits; IBM Condor ≈1,100).
Two precision points for SIGIL: (1) D-Wave's annealers cannot run Shor's algorithm at all —
invoking D-Wave for a factoring/signature threat is a category error; (2) a hash-chain's
ordering/tamper-evidence (SHA-256) is NOT broken by quantum — only the signature layer
needs migration. Forbes + 10
Q2c — Quantum ML for routing/optimization/benchmarking: REFUTED. The
dequantization literature (Ewin Tang; Cotler et al., arXiv:2112.00811) shows the purported
exponential advantages of QML on classical data are matched by classical algorithms with
sample-and-query access; genuine advantage is confined to quantum-native data and
contrived tasks, and QML models must be both trainable and non-dequantizable to matter. For
this venture's problems (routing, refusal scoring, governance benchmarking), QML offers no
practical advantage. Verdict: do not pursue.
Q3 — Photonic/optical "light as data": grand-slam hunch REFUTED for compute,
PARTIALLY-REAL for interconnect. Photonic compute (Lightmatter Envise, photonic tensor
cores, diffractive/analog ONNs — Feldmann Nature 589; Shen Nat. Photonics 11; Ahmed
Nature 640 2025) remains lab/hyperscaler-only. The 2025–2026 commercial products are co-
packaged optics/interconnect, not general compute: Lightmatter's Passage M1000 (press
release, 31 Mar 2025) claims "a record-breaking 114 Tbps total optical bandwidth" on a 4,000
mm² interposer with 256 fibers/1,024 SerDes; the Passage L200/L200X (34/64 Tbps) "enable
more than 200 Tbps of total I/O bandwidth per chip package, resulting in up to 8x faster
training time" (all vendor-reported bandwidth/speed figures, not independent benchmarks,
and not FLOPS). Light is genuinely superior for transmission (chip-to-chip data movement at
lower energy/latency); it is NOT a general-purpose faster/cheaper compute substrate for a solo
builder. "Turning fibreoptic light into data" = transceivers + coherent detection + DSP —
mature telecom engineering with nothing to "re-engineer" for an AI-governance stack.
Verdict: not viable now or in ~3 years for this venture. Nearest true thing: optical-interconnect
economics lower hyperscale inference cost over time, which strengthens the small-model+KB
thesis — as frontier serving gets cheaper, the durable moat must be governance/provenance,
not model size. In-sensor/edge optical preprocessing is a legitimate adjacent idea but not
reachable for a solo founder today. Lightmatter® + 4

Details
The C2PA PQC white space (the crown jewel). C2PA's signature list is exclusively classical
across all spec versions through v2.4: ES256/ES384/ES512 (ECDSA), EdDSA (Ed25519),
PS256/PS384/PS512 (RSA-PSS). The C2PA Implementation Guidance 2.2 (dated 2025-04-22)
§6.2 states verbatim: "C2PA recommends: ES256 (ECDSA with SHA-256) … EdDSA (Ed25519)
… or PS256 (RSASSA-PSS using SHA-256 …)" — no PQC option. No C2PA/CAI blog, GitHub
issue, or working-group roadmap commits to PQC migration, and no C2PA document
addresses "harvest now, decrypt later" versus signatures. Yet the enabling primitives now exist:
RFC 9964 (Prorock & Steele, published 22 May 2026) registered ML-DSA-44 (COSE alg −48),
ML-DSA-65 (−49), and ML-DSA-87 (−50) in the IANA COSE Algorithms registry (COSE is
exactly C2PA's COSE_Sign1 signing envelope), all marked "Recommended: Yes"; and RFC
9881 (Oct 2025) registered ML-DSA identifiers for X.509 (C2PA's certificate layer). Peer
ecosystems have already moved (Sigstore added ML-DSA; OpenSSF Model Signing supports
PQC selection; Google Cloud KMS offers ML-DSA/SLH-DSA). Because C2PA manifests are
designed to validate "indefinitely," an Ed25519-signed manifest is forgeable once a CRQC
exists — a documentable gap with no vendor mitigation. This is white space ProvBench can
occupy immediately and deterministically. (Practical note: ML-DSA-44 signatures are ≈2,420 B
vs Ed25519's 64 B — a manifest-size consideration, not a blocker.) C2pa + 6
n_eff and decorrelation. The measured quorum failure (n_eff 1.21 of 3, phi +0.743, retracted) is
exactly what correlation theory predicts when all three legs are prompt-variants of one 0.5B
Transformer: their errors are near-identical, so effective independent votes collapse toward 1.
The only real fix (task #18) is an architecturally distinct leg whose error structure decorrelates
from the Transformer. A state-space model (Mamba-2, RWKV-7, Falcon-Mamba) or linear-
attention model (Kimi Linear KDA — a finite-state recurrent memory with channel-wise
gating) has a fundamentally different inductive bias (recurrent finite-state vs full attention)
and thus different failure modes — the mechanism by which n_eff would rise above 1. This is a
hypothesis to falsify empirically (measure post-distillation inter-leg error correlation), not an
assumption to bank.
Quantum timelines (primary sources), anchored for a UK venture: Gidney+Ekerå 2019
(Quantum, 2021) — 20M qubits, 8 h. Gidney 2025 (arXiv:2505.15917) — <1M noisy qubits, 4.96
days. NIST IR 8547 (deprecate 2030 / disallow 2035). NSA CNSA 2.0 (support 2027; signing
exclusive by ~2030). UK NCSC (2028 / 2031 / 2035). These are planning anchors, not forecasts
— no CRQC exists. arXiv Quantum Journal
