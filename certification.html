Certification — what CSOAI measures and does not

CSOAI measures AI compliance with statute on published harnesses. CSOAI does not certify, accredit, or designate. The boundary is structural, not strategic. A measurement body and a certification body cannot be the same operation without breaking independence — the entire value of an independent measurement is that it can be consumed by an accredited certifier without conflict of interest.

What CSOAI is

  • A measurement body: publishes harnesses, runs them, signs the results, makes them recompute-able.
  • An evidence producer: a notified body, regulator, or counsel can take our signed records and use them as audit evidence.
  • An Ed25519 signer: every published figure carries a signature verifiable against /.well-known/agent.json.

What CSOAI is not

  • Not a certification body. A certification body is accredited by UKAS (UK) or an EU NAB, and operates under ISO/IEC 17065 plus ISO/IEC 42006 for AI. CSOAI is neither accredited nor applying.
  • Not a notified body. A notified body is designated by an EU Member State. They have statutory authority to issue CE marks. CSOAI has no such authority.
  • Not a regulator. Enforcement powers under the EU AI Act rest with national market-surveillance authorities and the AI Office. There is no mechanism to delegate them to a private company.
  • Not legal counsel. Nothing on this site is legal advice.

Why the boundary matters

Independence is the entire moat. A body that measures AND certifies has no independent voice — its clients pay for the certification, and the measurement follows the same money. An AI vendor that buys a "compliance rating" from the same body that will mark the regulator's file has bought a rubber stamp.

CSOAI's structural choice: we measure, we sign, we publish the measurements, and the regulator or auditor or counsel makes the conformity call. The measurement is the diagnostic; the conformity call is the regulatory decision.

How our measurements are used by others

  • Notified bodies can take our signed runs as part of their technical-file evidence. The signed record is verifiable; the conformity opinion is theirs.
  • Regulators can use our records as informational input. They remain the regulators.
  • Internal compliance teams can use our harnesses to test their own systems before filing with a notified body.
  • AI vendors can publish their CSOAI run beside their own declarations — a public, verifiable, signed record of what was measured.

Boundary

This is the law, not a marketing choice. If you wanted a certification, you need a certification body accredited under ISO/IEC 17065 plus (for AI) the ISO/IEC 42006 scheme. The lawful route to accreditation is documented, slow, and rigorous. We do not shortcut it; we do not pretend to hold it.
