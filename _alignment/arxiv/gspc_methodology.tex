\begin{abstract}
We present GSPC (Governance, Safety, Provenance, Continuity), a measurement
instrument for AI-governance evaluation, and the methodology behind a 14-slot
benchmark board of which 13 axes are currently measured. The design commits to
three principles that distinguish measurement from certification: (i)
deterministic grading against frozen, published gold labels---no model judges
another model; (ii) statistical honesty---a point-estimate lead is reported as
a tie unless it survives a McNemar test on discordant items ($p<0.05$), and
unparsed responses are reported as unmeasured rather than scored as wrong; and
(iii) cryptographic accountability---every published figure is recomputable
from signed, versioned rows (Ed25519), and corrections are appended, never
silently edited. Across a 19-model fleet on frozen splits, only 3 of 13
canonical axes exhibit a statistically separated leader (governance, care,
affect); the remaining 10 are statistical ties, including cases where the
instrument's own fine-tuned models do not lead. We anchor the corpus to a
frozen 417-provision statutory reference and release the item banks, scoring
code, and signed boards in full. We argue that an evaluation body's credibility
rests on publishing results that embarrass it---permanently, without
retraction---and we release the negative results and ties as first-class
outputs. Measurement, not certification: GSPC issues no conformity marks and
makes no regulatory determinations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
AI governance evaluation currently lacks an instrument that is simultaneously
\emph{anchored} (every score resolves to a frozen statutory provision),
\emph{deterministic} (no model judges another model), \emph{signed} (results
are recomputable evidence, not claims), and \emph{agentic} (harness-aware).
GSPC is our attempt to build that instrument. This paper describes the
methodology; the item banks, scoring code, and signed boards are released in
full at \url{https://huggingface.co/csoai} and \url{https://councilof.ai/api/gspc}.

\section{The instrument}
\subsection{The board}
GSPC maintains a 14-slot benchmark board of which 13 axes are currently
measured: Governance, Privacy, AGI Safety, ASI Preparedness, MCP Integrity,
OSS Compliance, Mechanized/Embodied, Care, XR/Immersive, Detection/Watermark,
Article~5, Swarm, and Affective Safety. The 14th slot (Human Baseline) is
measured against published aggregate human performance (e.g.\ MMLU expert
$\approx 89.8\%$, GPQA PhD-level), which is not human-subjects data and
therefore not subject to data-protection impact assessment for reuse.

\subsection{Deterministic grading}
Each item is graded by an exact-match predicate against a frozen gold label:
$exact\_match(G)$, $refusal(S)$, $action\_forbidden(S)$, $manifest\_valid(P)$,
$signature\_alg(C)$. No language model acts as judge. Partial credit and a
care cost apply to safety items. A response that cannot be parsed is reported
as \emph{unmeasured}, never scored as wrong.

\subsection{Statistical honesty}
A point-estimate lead is reported as a tie unless it survives a McNemar test
on discordant item pairs ($p<0.05$). All small-sample figures carry Wilson
score intervals; $n<20$ is labelled as a lower bound. This is why 10 of 13
axes report no separated leader even though point estimates differ.

\subsection{Cryptographic accountability}
Every published figure is recomputable from signed, versioned rows. Each row
carries an Ed25519 signature; the ledger is hash-chained; corrections are
appended as new entries, never silently edited. The card format is a signed
Q\&A capsule of approximately 3\,KB, recomputable by any third party offline.

\section{Results}
Across a 19-model fleet on frozen splits, only 3 of 13 canonical axes exhibit
a statistically separated leader: governance, care, and affect. On the
remaining 10 axes the field is a statistical tie. Notably, the instrument's own
fine-tuned models do not lead on any axis: in our 16-axis arena, the sovereign
fine-tune \texttt{council-oowm} scores below a base 4B model on every axis,
and our published arena league (2{,}900+ signed rounds) shows base models
occupying the top of the Elo ladder. We publish this result because an
evaluation body that hides its own losses cannot credibly measure others.

\section{Discussion}
The honest ceiling is stated on the tin: this instrument governs
\emph{provenance}, not \emph{correctness}. An attested answer is attested,
never verified. The moat is not secrecy but the simultaneous satisfaction of
four properties---anchored, deterministic, signed, agentic---which together
make every claim independently recomputable. We argue this is the correct
posture for a measurement body: verify free, forever, without asking.
