# NIS2 Essentials: What the Revised Network and Information Security Directive Means for AI Governance

**Meta Description:** The NIS2 Directive expands cybersecurity obligations to more sectors and introduces stricter supply chain security, incident reporting, and governance requirements — including for AI systems. Essential reading for regulated entities deploying MCP-based AI.

## Key Points
- NIS2 expands scope to 18+ sectors including healthcare, digital infrastructure, public administration, and food — with AI systems used in these sectors falling under its incident reporting and supply chain requirements.
- Key changes from NIS1: broader scope (medium/large entities in new sectors), shorter incident reporting timelines (24h early warning, 72h notification, 1-month final report), and personal liability for C-suite.
- Supply chain security (Art.21(2)(d)) requires entities to assess the cybersecurity of ICT suppliers — including AI model providers, MCP service vendors, and cloud inference platforms.
- Incident reporting obligations now capture AI-specific incidents: model poisoning, data leakage via MCP, adversarial attacks against deployed AI systems.
- Automated MCP attestation creates the evidence trail needed for NIS2 incident reports and supply chain security assessments.
