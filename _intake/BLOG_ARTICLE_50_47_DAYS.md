# 47 days until the Article 50 cliff. Are you compliant?

**Meta description:** 2 August 2026 is the EU AI Act's Article 50 enforcement date. Penalties of EUR 15M or 3% global turnover. What Annex III means and what to do now.

---

There are 47 days until the Article 50 deadline. If your AI system falls under Annex III of the EU AI Act, 2 August 2026 is not a planning milestone — it is an enforcement cliff. After that date, non-compliance carries penalties of EUR 15 million or 3% of your annual global turnover, whichever is higher. Per incident. Per jurisdiction.

Let me be direct. If you have not started your Article 50 compliance work, you cannot finish it in 47 days. The paperwork alone — risk assessment, technical documentation, human oversight protocols — takes a full cycle to generate and validate. But you can get far enough to demonstrate good faith, which matters more than most founders realise.

## What Article 50 actually says

Article 50 is the EU AI Act's transparency obligations chapter. It applies to any provider or deployer of an AI system listed in Annex III — the high-risk use cases. That includes:

- Biometric categorisation and remote identification systems
- Critical infrastructure management (transport, energy, water)
- Educational and vocational training access tools
- Employment, worker management, and self-employment access
- Access to essential services (credit, insurance, benefits)
- Law enforcement (risk assessment, evidence evaluation, lie detection)
- Migration, asylum, and border control
- Administration of justice and democratic processes

If your product scores applicants, ranks candidates, assesses creditworthiness, monitors workers, or makes eligibility decisions in any regulated domain, you are in scope. There is no "we are just an API layer" exception. If your model's output drives a decision in an Annex III use case, you are liable.

## What the deadline means

2 August 2026 is the date when Article 50's transparency obligations become enforceable across all EU member states. By that date, you must have:

1. **A conformity assessment** — a documented evaluation showing your system meets the Act's requirements for risk management, data governance, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, and cybersecurity. For most Annex III systems, this is a self-assessment (not third-party), but the documentation standard is detailed. You need version-controlled evidence, not a checklist.

2. **Human oversight protocols** — written procedures specifying who reviews the system's outputs, at what threshold, with what authority to override, and what training they have received. If a human signs off on 200 AI-generated decisions per hour, that is not oversight.

3. **Technical documentation** — the full specification: system design, development methodology, training data characteristics, performance metrics, known limitations, and intended purpose. The Act mandates this be kept up to date for the system's entire lifecycle.

4. **Transparency and user notification** — end users must be told they are interacting with an AI system. Not in the privacy policy. At the point of interaction.

## Penalties are not theoretical

EUR 15 million or 3% global turnover is the fine tier for transparency violations under Article 71(3). That is not the top tier — that is the middle tier. The top tier (7% or EUR 35M) applies to prohibited practices. The point is that EU regulators now have the tools to make AI non-compliance existential for mid-size companies.

If you are a Series B company doing EUR 50M in revenue, 3% is EUR 1.5M. That is a down round. That is headcount cuts. That is real.

## What to do between now and 2 August

47 days is not enough to build from scratch. Here is what you can do:

1. **Classify your systems.** Go through every product feature and ask: does this output affect a natural person's access to credit, employment, education, insurance, justice, or border crossing? If yes, it is Annex III. Document that classification decision with a timestamp.

2. **Draft your risk assessment.** The Act requires a risk identification and mitigation document. Template it now. Populate what you know. Flag gaps explicitly. A dated, good-faith risk assessment with acknowledged open items is far stronger in an enforcement context than a blank page.

3. **Review your oversight chain.** Ask: who actually reviews this system's outputs? Do they have the authority to stop production? Is that authority documented? If the answers are "engineers" / "no" / "no", you have a gap.

4. **Appoint a compliance lead.** Someone internal who owns the Article 50 filing. Not a consultant. A named person within your organisation who can testify to the documentation's accuracy.

47 days. Start today.
