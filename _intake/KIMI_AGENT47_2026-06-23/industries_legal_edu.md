## 1.5 Cross-Border Compliance Scenarios

### Scenario 1: EU-US Legal AI Data Transfer
A US-based legal AI company (e.g., Harvey AI) provides contract analysis services to a Magic Circle law firm in London. The AI processes client documents containing EU personal data. **Compliance challenges**: Must comply with EU AI Act (high-risk system requirements), GDPR (data transfer mechanisms - Standard Contractual Clauses, Data Protection Impact Assessments), and UK AI white paper principles. The US company faces the "Schrems II" decision implications for EU-US data transfers and must implement supplementary measures.

### Scenario 2: Cross-Border Litigation Analytics
A global litigation analytics platform aggregates court data from US (PACER), UK, EU, and Asian jurisdictions to predict case outcomes. **Compliance challenges**: Different access rules (US charges fees; EU requires "legitimate interest"; some jurisdictions restrict court data use), GDPR Article 85 journalistic exemption, varying levels of data anonymization, and the EU AI Act's high-risk classification for AI systems affecting judicial proceedings.

### Scenario 3: AI Judge in Singapore-Sanctioned System
Harvey AI collaborated with the Singapore judiciary to deploy an AI system in Small Claims Tribunals assisting common people navigating the legal system. **Compliance challenges**: Must align with Singapore's Model AI Governance Framework (voluntary), PDPA data protection requirements, and maintain human oversight as required by Singapore's judicial standards. Cross-border data flows between Singapore and US training data sources must comply with both jurisdictions.

## 2.5 Cross-Border Compliance Scenarios

### Scenario 1: AI Tutor Deployed Across EU and US Schools
An AI tutoring company deploys its platform in schools across Germany, France, and the US. The AI provides personalized learning paths and grades student work. **Compliance challenges**: EU AI Act classifies grading AI as HIGH-RISK (compliance by Aug 2027); GDPR children's data protections; FERPA compliance in US schools; COPPA for under-13 students; EU-US data transfer mechanisms (SCCs); divergent requirements for human oversight.

### Scenario 2: Cross-Border Language Learning Platform (Duolingo Model)
A language learning app with AI-powered features operates in 180+ countries, processing voice recordings and learning data from children and adults. **Compliance challenges**: COPPA (US), GDPR (EU), PIPL (China), LGPD (Brazil), DPDP (India) all apply to children's data with different consent requirements; voice recordings may be biometric data under some jurisdictions; algorithmic recommendation regulations in China; AI Basic Act requirements in South Korea.

### Scenario 3: Global University Using AI for Admissions
An international university with campuses in UAE, Singapore, UK, and Australia uses AI to screen applications and predict student success. **Compliance challenges**: EU AI Act HIGH-RISK classification for admissions AI; UK GDPR automated decision-making rules; Singapore PDPA consent requirements; UAE data localization requirements; Australian Privacy Act; obligation to provide explanations for rejected applications varies by jurisdiction.

## 6.5 Cross-Border Compliance Scenarios

### Scenario 1: AI Valuation Model Operating Across Multiple Countries
An AI-powered AVM company provides property valuations across US, UK, Germany, and Australia for mortgage lenders. **Compliance challenges**: US Fair Housing Act (bias testing requirements); UK GDPR for automated decision-making; EU AI Act potential HIGH-RISK classification for credit scoring; German property valuation regulations (very regulated market); Australian consumer protection for AI-generated valuations; different data sources and market characteristics in each jurisdiction.

### Scenario 2: Smart Building AI Across Multi-National Portfolio
A commercial real estate investment fund manages buildings across US, EU, Singapore, and UAE using AI for energy optimization, predictive maintenance, and tenant experience. **Compliance challenges**: EU AI Act for building management; EU GDPR for occupant data (biometrics, behavior tracking); Singapore PDPA for tenant data; UAE data localization requirements; US state-level biometric laws; cross-border transfer of building operational data; different energy efficiency standards.

### Scenario 3: PropTech Platform for Cross-Border Property Transactions
A platform facilitates international property purchases using AI for valuation, document verification, and contract generation across UK, Spain, UAE, and India. **Compliance challenges**: UK money laundering regulations; Spanish property transaction laws; UAE foreign ownership restrictions; India RERA compliance; cross-border payment processing regulations; AI-generated contracts validity varies by jurisdiction; KYC/AML requirements differ significantly.

## Key Cross-Border Compliance Challenges

1. **Regulatory Fragmentation**: No harmonized global AI framework; companies must comply with divergent requirements across jurisdictions
2. **Data Transfer Restrictions**: EU GDPR, China PIPL, India DPDP all impose cross-border data transfer restrictions with different mechanisms
3. **High-Risk AI Classification Divergence**: EU AI Act, South Korea AI Basic Act, and Brazil AI Bill use different risk classification systems
4. **Liability and Insurance**: No harmonized international framework for AI liability; product liability, negligence, and strict liability regimes vary
5. **Intellectual Property**: AI training data copyright issues unresolved globally; patent eligibility for AI inventions varies
6. **Human Oversight Requirements**: EU AI Act mandates human oversight for high-risk AI; other jurisdictions have varying requirements
7. **Algorithmic Transparency**: China requires algorithm filing; EU requires explainability; US has emerging state-level requirements
8. **Children's Data**: COPPA (US), GDPR (EU), LGPD (Brazil), DPDP (India) all have different children's data protections
