## 2.3 Regulations by Jurisdiction

### European Union
- **EU AI Act:** Humanoid robots with AI systems fall under high-risk if used in manufacturing, healthcare, or public services. Conformity assessment, risk management, human oversight required.
- **Machinery Regulation (2023/1230):** Physical safety requirements for robotic systems. Harmonized standards EN ISO 10218-1/2 for robot safety.
- **Product Liability Directive:** Extended to AI systems; presumption of defect for safety failures.

### United States
- **No federal humanoid-specific law.** OSHA workplace safety rules apply. State-level AI laws (Colorado, California, Texas) may apply to humanoid AI decision-making.
- **S. 3275: "Humanoid Robot Act of 2025"** - Proposed bill to ban federal procurement of Chinese humanoid robots.
- **NIST AI RMF** applicable for AI components. ISO/TS 15066 for collaborative robot safety.

### China
- **No specific humanoid robot law.** Governed by AI regulations (CAC measures), cybersecurity law, product safety standards.
- **GB/T 12643-2021:** National robot safety standards. MIIT promotes humanoid robot industry through national strategy.

### United Kingdom
- No specific humanoid legislation. Health and Safety Executive guidance on robot safety applies. Principles-based AI White Paper approach.

### South Korea
- **AI Basic Act (effective Jan 2026):** High-impact AI definition may cover humanoid robots in manufacturing/healthcare. Impact assessment required.
- **K-New Deal:** Government investment in AI and robotics as national priority.

### Japan
- **AI Promotion Act:** Promotes humanoid robot R&D; no binding restrictions. Japan leads in elderly care robot adoption.
- **ISO 13482 (service robot safety standard)** applicable.

### Other Jurisdictions
- All jurisdictions' AI regulations and data protection laws apply to humanoid robots with AI capabilities. Workplace safety regulations universally apply to industrial deployments.

## 3.3 Regulations by Jurisdiction

### United States
- **No comprehensive federal AV law.** NHTSA has voluntary guidance ("Automated Driving Systems 2.0" / "AV 3.0" / "AV 4.0").

- **State-level:** All 50 states have AV legislation. Key states:
  - **California:** Most restrictive; requires permits for testing and deployment from DMV and CPUC
  - **Arizona:** Open approach; minimal regulation ("regulatory sandbox")
  - **Texas:** AV-friendly; allows operation without driver; HB 2206 (2017) authorized AV testing
  - **Florida:** Permits AV testing without human driver (HB 311, 2019)
  - **Pennsylvania:** Authorized testing zones
- **TRUMP AMERICA AI Act (2026):** Proposed federal bill to preempt state AV laws.

### China
- **National Strategy:** "Intelligent Connected Vehicle" (ICV) national strategy. Baidu Apollo, AutoX, Pony.ai operate under local permits.
- **Regulations:** Local regulations in 20+ cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Guangzhou). National standards being developed.
- **Data Security Law:** Cross-border transfer of AV-collected geographic data restricted.
- **No comprehensive national AV law yet** - city-by-city permits.

### United Kingdom
- **Automated and Electric Vehicles Act 2018:** Insurance framework for AVs.
- **Code of Practice:** Trialling automated vehicles on public roads.
- **No specific AV legislation** - testing permitted under Code of Practice.

### Other Jurisdictions
- **Japan:** Level 4 operation permitted in designated areas since 2023. amended Road Transport Vehicle Act.
- **South Korea:** K-New Deal includes AV investment. Level 3 permitted on highways.
- **Singapore:** Autonomous vehicle testing zones (one-north, NTU). MAS guidelines for AV insurance.
- **UAE:** Dubai AV Strategy; testing permits available.
- **Australia:** No specific AV law; testing under state-level permits.
