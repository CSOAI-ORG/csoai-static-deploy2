## 1. Executive Summary

### Key Findings

The global AI safety governance landscape is **highly fragmented, under-resourced, and in flux**. No single body has comprehensive technical tooling, adequate staffing, or sufficient budget to effectively govern frontier AI systems. This creates a **massive opportunity for CSOAI** to serve as a unifying platform.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Total Organizations Analyzed** | 25+ |

3. **No Unified Data Layer**: Risk assessments, evaluations, and compliance data live in silos
4. **Limited Technical Integration**: Frameworks reference each other but don't integrate technically
5. **Enforcement Gaps**: Most frameworks are voluntary; enforcement mechanisms are weak or non-existent
6. **Compute Constraints**: Safety researchers lack access to frontier models and compute for evaluations
7. **Rapid Staffing Changes**: Political transitions (US AISI to CAISI) create instability

## 2. EU AI Office

### Overview
The **European Artificial Intelligence Office** (EU AI Office) is the most comprehensively resourced government AI regulatory body globally. Established within DG CONNECT of the European Commission on 21 February 2024, it serves as the EU-level market surveillance authority for General-Purpose AI (GPAI) models under the EU AI Act. [^2251^] [^2253^]

### Structure and Staffing

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Parent Organization** | DG CONNECT, European Commission |
| **Director** | Lucilla Sioli (PhD Economics, Southampton and Milan) |
| **Total Staff (2025)** | 125+ employees, targeting 140+ |
| **Initial Staff (2024)** | 60 transferred from existing DG CONNECT units |
| **Staff Composition** | Technology specialists, lawyers, policy specialists, economists, AI engineers |
| **Units** | 6 units + 2 advisors |

### Organizational Units [^2251^]

1. **CONNECT.A.1 - Excellence in AI & Robotics**: R&D excellence, innovation support
2. **CONNECT.A.2 - Regulation & Compliance**: Uniform application/enforcement of AI Act, investigations, coordination with member states
3. **CONNECT.A.3 - AI Safety**: GPAI model oversight, systemic risk assessment, capability evaluations
4. **CONNECT.A.4 - AI Innovation & Policy Coordination**: Innovation ecosystem, policy coherence
5. **CONNECT.A.5 - AI for Societal Good**: Societal benefit applications
6. **AI in Health and Life Science Unit**: Sector-specific oversight
7. **Lead Scientific Advisor**: Ensures scientific excellence in model evaluation
8. **International Affairs Advisor**: Global cooperation and alignment

### Budget and Resources

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Annual Budget (Unit A3 - Safety)** | ~EUR 20-30M estimated (report recommends EUR 50-60M by 2030) |
| **AI Office Total Budget** | Part of DG CONNECT allocation; exact figures not public |
| **Fining Power** | Up to EUR 35M or 7% global turnover for prohibited practices; up to EUR 15M or 3% for GPAI violations |
| **IT Infrastructure** | Developing; relies on Commission shared services |

**Critical Finding**: A Pour Demain report found Unit A3 (safety) is **under-resourced** relative to the number of frontier providers it must supervise. They recommend scaling to **160 staff by 2030** with a **EUR 50-60M annual budget**. [^2176^]

### Technical Tools and Capabilities

| Tool | Description | Status |
|------|-------------|--------|
| **GPAI Code of Practice** | Voluntary instrument for transparency, copyright, safety (developed with 1,000+ stakeholders) | Finalized July 2025 |
| **GPAI Guidelines** | Clarifies who must comply and how key concepts apply | Published 2025 |
| **Training Data Summary Template** | Template for providers to publish training data summaries | Published 2025 |
| **EU Database for High-Risk AI** | Central registry of high-risk AI systems (Art. 71) | In development |
| **Scientific Panel** | Independent expert panel for technical advice | Establishing |
| **Model Evaluation Capability** | Can conduct evaluations of GPAI models | Limited; building capacity |
| **Incident Investigation** | Power to investigate serious incidents and non-compliance | Framework developing |

### Key Gaps

1. **Severely Under-Resourced**: 125 staff to regulate the entire EU AI market is inadequate
2. **Limited Technical Infrastructure**: No dedicated compute cluster for model evaluation
3. **No Unified Software Platform**: Tools are policy documents, not integrated software
4. **Dependent on Member States**: National Market Surveillance Authorities enforce most provisions - creates fragmentation
5. **Lacks Real-Time Monitoring**: No capability for continuous model monitoring
6. **Limited Access to Frontier Models**: Must rely on provider self-reporting and voluntary cooperation
7. **80+ Implementation Acts Still Required**: Massive workload to operationalize the AI Act

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **VERY HIGH**

The EU AI Office is the **single most important integration target** for CSOAI:

- **Compliance Suite**: AI Act high-risk system obligations (effective Aug 2026) require risk management systems, post-market monitoring, and documentation - all areas where CSOAI's EAT could integrate
- **GPAI Oversight**: CSOAI could provide the technical infrastructure the AI Office lacks for model evaluation and monitoring
- **EU Database**: CSOAI could power the high-risk AI systems database
- **Standardized Reporting**: CSOAI reports could serve as standardized formats for AI Act compliance documentation
- **Cross-Border**: Platform approach aligns with EU's need for uniform enforcement across 27 member states

**Receptivity**: The EU AI Office is actively seeking technical solutions and partnerships. The Code of Practice process involved 1,000+ stakeholders, indicating openness to external tools. The Pour Demain report explicitly calls for additional resources and capacity building. [^2176^]

## 3. NIST AI Risk Management Framework

### Overview
The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, released January 2023; AI RMF 2.0 in development) is the world's most widely referenced voluntary AI risk management standard. It provides a structured approach to managing AI risks across the system lifecycle. [^2101^] [^2108^]

### Core Framework

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Functions** | GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE |
| **Target Users** | All AI actors - developers, deployers, evaluators |
| **Voluntary** | Non-binding guidance (key limitation) |
| **Crosswalks** | Maps to ISO/IEC 42001, NIST CSF 2.0, OECD AI Principles |
| **Generative AI Profile** | NIST AI 600-1 provides specific guidance for GenAI |

### Technical Tools

| Tool | Description | Status | Gaps |
|------|-------------|--------|------|
| **Dioptra** | Open-source adversarial evaluation platform; benchmarks against evasion, poisoning, extraction attacks via containerized workflows | Active development; praised by Microsoft/Google engineers | Limited adoption; small team maintaining |
| **Cyber-AI Overlays** | Translates SP 800-53 cybersecurity controls into AI-specific guidance | Draft | Not finalized; limited practical use |
| **Managing Misuse Risk Draft** | Guidance for dual-use foundation models | Draft | Voluntary only; no enforcement |
| **AI RMF Profiles** | Use-case profiles, temporal profiles, cross-sectoral profiles | Available | Organizations must build their own |
| **Crosswalks to ISO 42001** | Mapping for certification alignment | Published | Complex to implement |

### Critical Gaps [^2111^] [^2101^]

1. **Voluntary Nature**: No enforcement power; limited adoption incentive
2. **No Security Testing Protocols**: Lacks offensive/defensive security testing for AI systems [^2111^]
3. **No Specific Technical Requirements**: Does not prescribe encryption, access control, or differential privacy standards
4. **Deployment Gap**: Focuses on development, insufficient guidance for organizations purchasing/licensing AI tools
5. **No Tactical User Awareness Guidance**: Missing guidance on AI-specific user training and acceptable use policies
6. **Resource Disparities**: Smaller firms lack staff to operationalize technical standards
7. **Model Theft**: Lacks targeted strategies against model extraction attacks
8. **Measurement Burden**: Difficult for organizations to implement at scale

### Staffing and Budget

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **NIST Total Budget** | ~$1.5B annually (chronically underfunded relative to mandate) |
| **AI-specific allocation** | Embedded within ITL; no dedicated AI budget line |
| **Dioptra Team** | Small research team |
| **AI RMF Development** | Led by Elham Tabassi (CTO, US AISI/CAISI) and team |

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **HIGH**

- **Operationalize the RMF**: CSOAI's EAT framework directly maps to NIST AI RMF functions (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE)
- **Automated Compliance**: CSOAI could automate RMF profile creation and gap analysis
- **Dioptra Integration**: CSOAI could integrate Dioptra evaluations into its platform
- **Gap Filling**: CSOAI addresses NIST's key gaps: security testing, deployment guidance, automated measurement
- **Scale Reach**: NIST RMF is referenced globally; integration would give CSOAI massive credibility

**Receptivity**: NIST actively seeks implementation tools and has a history of partnering with industry (AISIC has 280+ members). The shift from CAISI back toward standards work under the Trump administration may increase openness. [^2100^]

## 4. UK AI Safety Institute (UK AISI)

### Overview
The UK AI Safety Institute (now **AI Security Institute**) was established in November 2023 as the world's first state-backed AI safety body. It sits within the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT). In February 2025, it was renamed to reflect a stronger national security focus. [^2104^] [^2110^]

### Structure and Staffing

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Established** | November 2023 |
| **Parent** | Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) |
| **Budget** | GBP 100M over 2 years (~$127M) |
| **Staff** | 100+ total; 30+ technical researchers |
| **CTO** | Jade Leung (appointed 2024) |
| **Chair** | Ian Hogarth |
| **International Offices** | London + San Francisco office |

### Key Technical Tool: Inspect [^2110^] [^2116^]

**Inspect** is the UK's flagship open-source contribution to AI safety - the first government-backed AI evaluation platform.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Type** | Open-source Python framework (MIT license) |
| **Purpose** | Building and running reproducible LLM evaluations |
| **Core Components** | Datasets (test scenarios), Solvers (execution), Scorers (aggregation) |
| **Capabilities** | Core knowledge testing, reasoning evaluation, autonomous capability assessment |
| **Isolation** | Docker built-in, optional Kubernetes/Proxmox adapters |
| **Logging** | JSONL, Postgres, Dash UI |
| **IDE Integration** | VS Code log viewer, web-based Inspect View |
| **Extensibility** | Third-party packages via Python |
| **Install** | `pip install inspect-ai` |

### Technical Capabilities

| Capability | Detail |
|------------|--------|
| **Pre-deployment Evaluations** | Joint evaluations with US AISI (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, OpenAI o1) |
| **Vulnerability Research** | Published first technical blog on model vulnerabilities (April 2024) |
| **International Scientific Report** | Led report on Advanced AI Safety with 30 countries, chaired by Yoshua Bengio |
| **Framework Development** | Inspect platform; evaluation methodologies |
| **Research Partnerships** | Canadian AISI, US AISI, METR, Apollo Research |

### Key Partnerships [^2104^]

- **Canada AISI**: Formal partnership with secondments and joint systemic safety research
- **US AISI/CAISI**: Bilateral agreement for joint safety testing (April 2024)
- **International Network**: Co-chair of 11-nation International Network of AI Safety Institutes
- **Academic/Research**: METR, Apollo Research, other safety labs

### Key Gaps

1. **Renamed/Refocused**: Shift from "safety" to "security" may narrow scope
2. **Inspect Adoption**: While powerful, Inspect is primarily used by technical researchers; not accessible to non-technical regulators
3. **Limited Staff**: 100 staff vs. multi-trillion-dollar AI industry
4. **No Enforcement Power**: Advisory role only; relies on voluntary cooperation
5. **No Integrated Compliance Platform**: Evaluations are separate from governance workflows
6. **Limited Compute**: Must rely on partnerships for frontier model access
7. **Focus on Frontier Models**: Less attention to deployed systems and enterprise AI

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **VERY HIGH**

- **Inspect Integration**: CSOAI could build a user-friendly interface layer on top of Inspect
- **Joint Evaluations**: Platform could standardize evaluation formats across UK-US and international partnerships
- **Accessibility**: Make Inspect's capabilities accessible to non-technical regulators
- **Compliance Pipeline**: Connect evaluations to compliance reporting
- **Data Sharing**: Facilitate secure data sharing between AISI and international partners

**Receptivity**: The UK AISI has demonstrated strong commitment to open-source and international cooperation. Inspect being open-source signals willingness to collaborate. The San Francisco office suggests active engagement with the AI ecosystem. [^2104^] [^2110^]

## 5. US AI Safety Institute (US AISI / CAISI)

### Overview
The US AI Safety Institute was established in November 2023 within NIST. In June 2025, it was renamed to **Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI)** following the Trump administration's revocation of Executive Order 14110. This represents the most significant political disruption in AI safety governance globally. [^2100^] [^2107^]

| Date | Event | Impact |

| Feb 2024 | Elizabeth Kelly appointed Director | Leadership in place |
| Feb 2024 | AISIC launched (200+ members) | Consortium building |
| Mar 2024 | $10M budget allocated | Severely underfunded |
| Aug 2024 | MOUs with OpenAI and Anthropic | Pre-deployment access secured |
| Nov 2024 | First joint US-UK evaluation (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) | Operational capability |
| Jan 2025 | EO 14110 revoked | Legal foundation removed |
| Feb 2025 | Elizabeth Kelly departs | Leadership vacuum |
| Feb 2025 | NIST layoffs announced (497 probationary employees) | Staff cuts |
| Mar 2025 | 73 NIST staff fired (~half later reinstated) | Institutional disruption |
| Jun 2025 | Renamed to CAISI | Mission shift from safety to standards/innovation |

### Structure and Resources

### Key Members of AISIC [^2100^]

### Technical Tools

| **AISIC Knowledge Sharing** | Consortium-based information exchange | Active |
| **International Coordination** | Co-chair of International Network of AISIs | Role uncertain post-renaming |

### Critical Gaps

The chaos at US AISI/CAISI creates both risk and opportunity:

- **AISIC Channel**: 280+ member consortium is a ready market
- **Standards Alignment**: CAISI's standards focus aligns with CSOAI's compliance capabilities
- **Bipartisan Appeal**: Platform approach can serve both safety and innovation agendas
- **State-Level Entry**: Federal disruption means states may fill the gap - CSOAI can serve state regulators

**Receptivity**: Currently low due to chaos, but the 280+ AISIC members remain engaged. NIST's standards mission is institutional and survives administrations. The need for technical capacity is greater than ever. [^2100^] [^2105^] [^2115^]

## 6. Singapore IMDA & AI Verify

### Overview
Singapore's Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) has developed the **world's most complete national AI governance toolkit stack**: AI Verify (governance testing) + Project Moonshot (LLM evaluation). This represents the most practically implementable government AI safety infrastructure globally. [^2102^] [^2103^] [^1883^]

### AI Verify

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Type** | Open-source AI governance testing framework and toolkit |
| **Developer** | IMDA (first government-developed AI testing toolkit globally) |
| **License** | Open-source (since June 2023) |
| **Governance** | AI Verify Foundation (neutral not-for-profit) |
| **Principles** | 11 testable principles aligned with EU, OECD, Singapore frameworks |
| **Data Privacy** | Runs entirely within enterprise environment; no data leaves |
| **Assessment Type** | Voluntary; no pass/fail; generates standardized reports |

#### AI Verify Technical Components [^1883^]

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Technical Tests** | Software-executed against AI models directly; fairness metrics (statistical parity, equal opportunity); explainability (SHAP, feature importance); robustness tests (input perturbation); supports tabular and image classification |
| **Process Checks** | Documentary evidence validation; risk assessment evaluation; checklist-based against 11 principles; covers transparency, accountability, safety where automated testing isn't feasible |
| **Standardized Report** | Combined output shareable with boards, regulators, customers |

#### AI Verify Foundation Members [^1883^] [^2254^]

| Category | Members |
|----------|---------|
| **Premier (9)** | AWS, Dell, Google, IBM, IMDA, Microsoft, Red Hat, Resaro, Salesforce |
| **General (180+)** | Adobe, Meta, DBS, Singapore Airlines, SenseTime, NTT Data, Grab, UOB, OCBC, Accenture, Bosch, Lenovo |

### Project Moonshot [^2103^] [^2106^]

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Type** | Open-source LLM evaluation toolkit |
| **Launch** | Open beta, 31 May 2024 |
| **Scope** | Red-teaming, benchmarking, baseline testing for LLMs |
| **Target Users** | Non-technical users (easy-to-understand results) |
| **Key Features** | 100+ benchmark datasets; automated adversarial testing; hallucination detection; prompt injection testing; multilingual/cultural red-teaming |
| **Integration** | Complements AI Verify (traditional AI) to form complete testing stack |

### Singapore's AI Governance Ecosystem

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Model AI Governance Framework** | National AI governance framework (finalized May 2024 for GenAI) |
| **AI Verify** | Governance testing toolkit |
| **Project Moonshot** | LLM evaluation toolkit |
| **AI Verify Foundation** | Open-source community governance |
| **Practical Guide to Multilingual Red Teaming** | First practical guide for multilingual/cultural LLM testing |

### Key Gaps

1. **Voluntary Only**: No mandatory enforcement; Singapore relies on soft law
2. **Limited to Traditional AI + LLMs**: Does not cover agentic AI, multi-modal systems, robotics
3. **No Real-Time Monitoring**: Point-in-time assessment, not continuous
4. **Limited International Reach**: Primarily Singapore-focused despite open-source availability
5. **No Cross-Border Recognition**: AI Verify reports not formally recognized by other jurisdictions
6. **Enterprise-Focused**: Less suited for government/internal AI use cases
7. **No Compute for Evaluations**: Users must provide their own compute

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **VERY HIGH**

Singapore is the **most practically advanced** AI governance regime and the most receptive to partnership:

- **Toolkit Integration**: CSOAI could integrate AI Verify and Project Moonshot into its platform
- **Cross-Border Bridge**: Singapore's position between East and West makes it ideal for CSOAI's cross-border features
- **180+ Member Network**: AI Verify Foundation members are potential CSOAI users
- **Complementary Stack**: AI Verify covers governance + Moonshot covers LLMs; CSOAI adds risk scoring, compliance automation, and cross-border data sharing
- **Government Credibility**: IMDA partnership would validate CSOAI globally

**Receptivity**: Singapore has explicitly positioned itself as a collaborative, open-source, internationally interoperable AI governance hub. Minister Josephine Teo stated "the government is no longer simply a regulator or experimenter. We are a full participant of the AI revolution." [^2259^]

## 7. Japan AI Governance & Hiroshima Process

### Overview
Japan has pioneered a "Third Way" in AI governance - softer than the EU's binding regulation but more structured than US voluntary approaches. The **Hiroshima AI Process** (launched G7 2023) and the **AI Safety Institute** (planned) are Japan's key contributions. [^2173^] [^2174^]

### Hiroshima AI Process [^2173^] [^2177^] [^2179^]

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Launched** | May 2023 at G7 Hiroshima Summit |
| **Scope** | International framework for safe, secure, trustworthy advanced AI |
| **Participation** | 66 countries + 38 organizations (Friends Group) |
| **Core Documents** | International Guiding Principles + International Code of Conduct |
| **Reporting** | Voluntary Reporting Framework (OECD-led, 20+ organizations publishing) |

### Japan's Domestic AI Governance Structure

| Component | Status |
|-----------|--------|
| **AI Safety Institute** | Planned; research on safety evaluation methods |
| **AI Promotion Act** | Enacted June 4, 2025; aligns with HAIP |
| **Tokyo Center of GPAI** | Planned; research on generative AI |
| **AI Strategy Council** | Chaired by Prof. Matsuo Yutaka (University of Tokyo) |

### Key Gaps

1. **No Enforcement Mechanism**: Entirely voluntary framework
2. **AI Safety Institute Not Yet Operational**: Still in planning phase
3. **Limited Technical Tools**: No dedicated evaluation software
4. **Soft Law Limitations**: "Aiming to share information" vs. mandatory reporting
5. **Dependent on OECD**: Reporting framework led by OECD; Japan provides political leadership but limited technical capacity
6. **No Dedicated Budget**: Unclear funding for AI Safety Institute

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **HIGH**

- **HAIP Reporting**: CSOAI could serve as a platform for HAIP transparency reporting
- **AI Safety Institute Support**: CSOAI could provide technical infrastructure as Japan builds out its institute
- **Bridge EU-Japan**: Japan's "Third Way" seeks interoperability - CSOAI can bridge EU AI Act and HAIP
- **GPAI Tokyo Center**: Research partnership opportunity
- **66-Country Network**: HAIP Friends Group is a massive potential user base

**Receptivity**: Japan has explicitly prioritized interoperability and practical implementation over prescriptive regulation. The HAIP framework's voluntary, collaborative nature aligns with CSOAI's platform approach. [^2173^] [^2174^]

## 8. Canada Directive on Automated Decision-Making

### Overview
Canada's **Directive on Automated Decision-Making** (effective April 2019, with compliance required by April 2020) was one of the world's first binding government AI governance frameworks. It applies to all federal government AI systems that affect Canadians. [^2125^] [^2128^]

### Technical Tool: Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) [^2132^]

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Type** | Open-source questionnaire (MIT License) |
| **Questions** | ~80 questions |
| **Time Required** | Minimum 35 minutes |
| **Scope** | Business process, data, system design, algorithm, decision |
| **Output** | Impact level classification (Level I - lowest to Level IV - highest) |
| **Data Storage** | No data stored; browser-based with JSON save/upload |
| **License** | Open source (MIT) |

### Classification Levels

| Level | Requirements |
|-------|-------------|
| **Level I** (Minimal) | Basic transparency |
| **Level II** (Low) | Enhanced documentation |
| **Level III** (Moderate) | Additional safeguards, peer review |
| **Level IV** (High) | Maximum safeguards, independent validation, human oversight |

### Key Gaps

1. **Limited to Federal Government**: Does not cover provincial, municipal, or private sector
2. **No Technical Testing**: AIA is questionnaire-based; no automated model evaluation
3. **Limited Enforcement**: Compliance within departments; no external enforcement body
4. **No Integration with Development**: Assessment is separate from model development lifecycle
5. **Static Tool**: No continuous monitoring or post-deployment assessment
6. **Limited International Recognition**: Not widely adopted outside Canada
7. **Narrow Scope**: Only covers automated decision-making, not all AI systems

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

- **Modernize the AIA**: CSOAI could provide automated, continuous assessment capabilities
- **Expand Scope**: Platform could extend beyond federal government to provincial/municipal and private sector
- **Technical Integration**: Add actual model testing to Canada's questionnaire-based approach
- **Canada AISI Partnership**: Canada has formal partnership with UK AISI - CSOAI could serve as technical infrastructure
- **Commonwealth Entry**: Canada provides entry point to other Commonwealth countries

**Receptivity**: Canada's partnership with UK AISI indicates openness to international collaboration. The AIA being open-source shows commitment to shared tooling. [^2125^] [^2128^] [^2104^]

## 23. Frontier Model Forum

### Overview
The Frontier Model Forum (FMF) is an industry body established in 2023 by Microsoft, Google DeepMind, OpenAI, and Anthropic to advance AI safety research and promote responsible development practices. [^2198^]

### Workstreams and Outputs

| Workstream | Output |
|------------|--------|
| **Frontier AI Frameworks** | Components of Safety Frameworks, Thresholds for Frontier AI Safety, Risk Taxonomy, Pre-Deployment Evaluation Taxonomy |
| **Safety Research** | Public library of technical evaluations and benchmarks |
| **Information Sharing** | Trusted mechanisms for companies and governments to share safety information |

### Key Publications [^2197^] [^2196^]

| Publication | Date |
|-------------|------|
| Components of Safety Frameworks | Nov 2024 |
| Thresholds for Frontier AI Safety Frameworks | Feb 2025 |
| Frontier Capability Assessments | Apr 2025 |
| Risk Taxonomy and Thresholds | Jun 2025 |
| Preliminary Taxonomy of Pre-Deployment Safety Evaluations | Jan 2025 |
| Early Best Practices for Frontier AI Safety Evaluations | Jul 2024 |

### Key Gaps

1. **Industry Self-Regulation**: No external oversight or enforcement
2. **Closed Membership**: Only frontier labs; excludes civil society and government
3. **Voluntary Only**: All commitments are voluntary
4. **Limited Technical Output**: Primarily guidance documents, not operational tools
5. **Coordination Problems**: Labs may lower standards collectively if one does [^2123^]
6. **No Independent Verification**: Self-reported evaluations only
7. **Competitive Dynamics**: Members are competitors; limits information sharing

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **HIGH**

- **Standardization Hub**: CSOAI could host FMF standards as operational tools
- **Independent Verification**: Provide third-party validation of FMF framework adherence
- **Government Interface**: Bridge FMF industry frameworks with government requirements
- **Research Integration**: Connect FMF research to practical governance tools
- **Expanded Membership**: CSOAI could serve as platform for broader stakeholder participation

**Receptivity**: FMF has called for "shared standards and best practices" - CSOAI provides implementation. The Forum's emphasis on "trusted, secure mechanisms for sharing information" aligns with CSOAI's platform. [^2198^] [^2197^]

## 24. International Network of AI Safety Institutes

### Overview
The International Network of AI Safety Institutes was formally launched at the AI Seoul Summit in May 2024. It represents the primary multilateral coordination mechanism for government AI safety bodies. [^2100^]

### Membership (as of 2024)

| Members | 11 founding nations |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Co-Chairs** | US AISI/CAISI, UK AISI |
| **Members** | US, UK, EU, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy |
| **Planned Expansion** | Ongoing |

### Activities

| Activity | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Joint Evaluations** | US-UK joint evaluation of Claude 3.5 Sonnet (Nov 2024) |
| **Standard Setting** | Coordination on safety evaluation standards |
| **Information Sharing** | Cross-border sharing of safety information |
| **Research Collaboration** | Joint research projects |
| **Plenary Meetings** | First AISIC plenary at University of Maryland (Dec 2024) |

### Key Gaps

1. **Limited to 11 Nations**: Many major AI players not included (China, India, Israel, UAE)
2. **No Shared Technical Platform**: Information sharing is ad hoc, not systematic
3. **No Binding Commitments**: Coordination is voluntary
4. **Political Disruption**: US withdrawal of EO 14110 destabilized the network
5. **Limited Staff Coordination**: No dedicated secretariat
6. **No Joint Tools**: Each institute develops its own tools independently
7. **Ad Hoc Meetings**: No systematic coordination mechanism

### CSOAI Integration Opportunity: **VERY HIGH**

This is the **highest-impact integration opportunity** for CSOAI:

- **Shared Platform**: CSOAI could serve as the technical backbone for the Network
- **Cross-Border Data Sharing**: Platform enables secure information sharing between institutes
- **Standardized Evaluations**: Common evaluation formats across all member institutes
- **Joint Dashboard**: Real-time monitoring of AI risks across jurisdictions
- **Secretariat Support**: Platform tools for coordination, scheduling, reporting
- **Expansion Enabler**: Lower barriers for new countries to join by providing ready infrastructure

**Receptivity**: The Network was created because members recognized the need for coordination. The lack of shared technical infrastructure is a known gap. Political transitions make a neutral platform (like CSOAI) more valuable. [^2100^]
