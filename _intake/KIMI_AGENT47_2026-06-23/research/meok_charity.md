# MEOK Charity + Gaming Integration Research

## Executive Summary

The gaming industry has emerged as one of the most powerful forces in charitable fundraising, with billions of dollars raised across multiple models. This research examines 15+ proven models where games partner with charities to generate real-world impact. From Humble Bundle's $280M+ pay-what-you-want model to Games Done Quick's $50M+ speedrunning marathons, to individual charity streamers raising $10M+ each, the evidence is overwhelming: gaming communities are exceptionally generous when given the right mechanisms to give.

**Key Findings:**

- **$50M+** raised by Games Done Quick since 2010 [^732^]
- **$25M+** raised by Jacksepticeye's Thankmas streams [^854^]

## 1. Humble Bundle — Pay-What-You-Want Charity Model

### Overview
Humble Bundle pioneered the "pay-what-you-want" charity game bundle model in 2010. Founded by Jeff Rosen and John Graham of Wolfire Games, it started as a way to bundle indie games and let customers choose their price, with proceeds split between developers, charity, and Humble Bundle itself.

### How Much Raised
- **$280 million+** raised for charity since 2010 [^745^]
- **$266 million+** total cumulative as of 2024 [^731^]
- **$12.4 million** raised in 2024 alone [^731^]
- **$14.4 million+** raised in 2023 [^743^]
- Supported **4,500+ charities** in 2024 [^731^]
- **$250 million** milestone reached in February 2024 [^744^]

### Engagement Model
1. **Pay-What-You-Want**: Customers choose their price for game bundles
2. **Charity Slider**: Buyers can adjust what percentage goes to charity (up to 100%)
3. **Humble Choice**: Monthly subscription service with charity component
4. **Humble Store**: Regular storefront with charity donations on every purchase

### Five Core Impact Pillars
1. **Health & Well-being** ($2.3M for disease prevention/research in 2024)
2. **Crisis & Disaster Relief** ($1.2M in 2024)
3. **Equity & Opportunity** (Girls Make Games, ESA Foundation, XPerience Studios)
4. **Quality Education** (Girls Who Code, Hidden Genius Project)
5. **Climate Change & Sustainability**

### Gaming Industry Impact (2024)
- **SpecialEffect**: 100 bespoke assistive controllers + 100 eye-tracking setups for disabled players
- **Child's Play**: Games for therapeutic purposes in US and UK hospitals
- **Girls Make Games Scholarship Fund**: 20 summer camp scholarships, 800 workshop scholarships, reaching 15,000 students
- **Safe In Our World, Take This, Crisis Text Line**: Mental health support in gaming
- **4,577,744 meals** provided in 2024 [^731^]

### Key Quote
> "From day one everyone at Humble has wanted to find ways to give back through our work and it's all the more rewarding when we can see the impact hitting even closer to home." — Kamini Tiwari, VP Social Impact, Humble Bundle [^731^]

## 3. Charity Skins — Pink Mercy & In-Game Cosmetics for Good

### Overview
Charity skins are limited-edition cosmetic items sold in-game with 100% of proceeds donated to charity. The Pink Mercy campaign is the gold standard, raising unprecedented amounts.

### Pink Mercy (Overwatch) — 2018
- **$12.7 million** raised for Breast Cancer Research Foundation [^736^]
- Skin priced at **$14.99** (now $15)
- **100% of proceeds** donated (minus platform fees/taxes)
- Sold for **2 weeks** (May 8-21, 2018)
- **820,000+ people** purchased the skin [^750^]
- Single largest donation ever received by BCRF at that time [^740^]

### Pink Mercy Returns — 2024
- **$12.3 million** raised for BCRF (June 25 - July 8, 2024) [^735^]
- Added new **Rose Gold Mercy Bundle** ($19.99)
- Cumulative total: **$25 million+** for BCRF across both campaigns [^742^]
- Second-largest single corporate donation to BCRF [^735^]

### Funded Research
- Advanced AI methods for mammogram analysis
- Predictive models for triple-negative breast cancer
- Gene classifier for DCIS risk assessment
- Treatments to delay tumor formation [^735^]

### Why It Works
- **Limited-time urgency**: 2-week window creates FOMO
- **Desirable cosmetic**: Premium quality skin players actually want
- **100% transparency**: Clear messaging that all proceeds go to charity
- **No in-game currency**: Must be purchased with real money at retail price [^740^]
- **Streaming integration**: BCRF provided Tiltify and Streamlabs overlays for streamers to amplify [^740^]

## 12. In-Game Donation Mechanics

### Overview
Modern games have integrated donation mechanics directly into gameplay, making giving part of the player experience rather than an external action.

### Models

#### 1. Direct Purchase Donation (100% Proceeds)
- **Pink Mercy**: 100% of purchase price to BCRF [^740^]
- **WoW Charity Pets**: 100% to designated charity [^751^]
- **Reven Pack**: 100% to CureDuchenne [^751^]
- **Fortnite Ukraine**: 100% of all Fortnite proceeds to Ukraine relief [^843^]
- Transparent pricing with clear disclosure of where money goes

#### 2. In-Game Currency Donation
- **Fortnite Donation Stations**: Players donate Bars (in-game currency) to unlock community rewards [^786^]
- Collective goal system — all players benefit when goal reached
- Gamifies giving by rewarding participation

#### 3. Bundle-for-Charity
- **Humble Bundle**: Pay-what-you-want with adjustable charity split [^745^]
- **itch.io charity bundles**: "Bundle for Ukraine" raised 4.7M+ pounds offering 1,000 games [^789^]
- **Humble Ukraine Bundle**: $10M+ raised with 120+ games [^789^]

#### 4. Time-Based Monetization
- **#TheDonationMap**: Players generate donations by staying on the Fortnite map [^790^]
- Map earns money based on play time, donated to charity
- No purchase required from players

#### 5. Optional Checkout Donation
- **WoW Pet Pack for Ukraine**: Optional donation at checkout on Battle.net [^758^]
- Allows support without requiring item purchase

### Best Practices
1. **100% transparency**: Clearly state what percentage goes to charity
2. **Limited-time exclusivity**: Create urgency with time-bound availability
3. **Desirable items**: Charity items must be high-quality and appealing
4. **Platform integration**: Work with Tiltify, Streamlabs for streamer amplification
5. **Multi-channel**: Combine in-game, streaming, and social media

## 13. Play-to-Donate Models

### Overview
Play-to-donate models allow players to contribute to charity simply by playing games — no direct donation required. Sponsors fund the donation based on player engagement.

### Key Examples

#### Freerice (World Food Programme)
- **Created**: 2008 by John Breen, donated to WFP in 2010 [^802^]
- **Mechanic**: Players answer trivia questions; each correct answer = 10 grains of rice donated
- **8 million players per year** [^802^]
- **214 billion grains of rice** earned since 2010 (equivalent to **$1.5 million**) [^802^]
- **60+ categories**: English Vocabulary, Multiplication, SDGs, Women Leaders, Pop Culture
- **Sponsor-funded**: Private sponsors pay for the rice; players' engagement unlocks donations

#### PlanetPlay
- **$2.2 million** driven for environmental projects in first year [^813^]
- **Play2Act**: In-game survey initiative with UNDP
  - 200,000+ players across 190 countries [^813^]
  - Captures insights on climate, sustainability, social challenges
  - Contributes data to UNDP's SparkBlue platform [^813^]
- **PlayForEveryChild**: PC Games Collection launching October 2026 with UNICEF [^808^]
- 100% of net profits from game sales donated

#### Ecosia + Gaming
- Search engine that plants trees with every search
- Integrated into Green Game Jam activations [^801^]
- Gaming companies direct players to use Ecosia
- **2.4+ million trees planted** through Green Game Jam 2022 [^801^]

### Why It Works
- **Zero cost to player**: Removes financial barrier entirely
- **Engagement incentive**: Players feel good while doing something they'd do anyway
- **Sponsor model**: Corporate sponsors fund donations in exchange for brand exposure
- **Scalable**: No upper limit on participation
- **Educational**: Freerice teaches while it fundraises
